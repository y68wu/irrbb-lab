WEEK 01 / Oct 11–Oct 17

Build the opening book

Define the modeled bank, benchmark the rate curve and establish a reproducible repricing schedule.
DAY 01Oct 11 · Sun

Set the IRRBB scope

Represent a synthetic Canadian-dollar banking book and measure two different exposures. Economic value of equity is the discounted value of asset cash flows less funding cash flows, plus the swap. Net interest income is earned interest less funding expense over a horizon.

Check. Keep the opening valuation, shocked valuation, and 12-month earnings path separate in the report. State that the balance sheet is illustrative.

Scope and limitations ↗
DAY 02Oct 12 · Mon

Map a bank’s published categories

Use public RBC and TD balance-sheet disclosures to anchor the standalone HELOC share in the modeled secured retail book. Create a CAD 100B synthetic asset book and fund it with CAD 93B of deposits, GICs and wholesale borrowing. Reserve CAD 7B as the illustrative Tier 1 capital input.

Check. Reconcile the opening assets and funding to the assumed equity. Keep book equity separate from the illustrative Tier 1 capital used in the screen.

Public inputs and balance-sheet assumptions ↗
DAY 03Oct 13 · Tue

Capture a dated Government of Canada curve

Retrieve official zero-coupon yields, preserve the observation date and keep maturities in years. The Bank of Canada estimates these curves from bond and treasury bill prices and ordinarily publishes the data with a lag. The default observation is October 21, 2026.

Check. Verify that observations are ordered, rates are in decimal form, and the published observation date is distinct from the date the project is viewed.

Bank of Canada zero curves ↗
DAY 04Oct 14 · Wed

Make the curve reproducible

Use a repeatable refresh step to capture the source inputs and feed a pinned curve snapshot to both the browser and the reporting run. Add three historical opening curves to isolate sensitivity to curve levels and shapes while holding the modeled balance sheet fixed.

Check. Confirm that an exact input knot reproduces its original value and that the refresh retains its source URL and response hash. Describe historical curve comparisons as controlled experiments.

Curve refresh script ↗
DAY 05Oct 15 · Thu

Read the Canadian supervisory calibration

Use the Canadian column in the revised guidance. The parallel, short and long shock sizes are 200, 275 and 175 bp respectively. The post-shock curve uses the OSFI floor of −75 bp.

Check. Use the final B-12 published September 10, 2026. Its effective dates are November 1, 2026 for October fiscal year-ends and January 1, 2027 for December fiscal year-ends.

Final OSFI B-12 and effective dates ↗
DAY 06Oct 16 · Fri

Translate products into repricing buckets

Place floating positions at their next reset. Allocate fixed-rate positions and runoff by expected timing. Split non-maturity deposits into a short-lived component and a modeled core component to avoid placing their entire balance at contractual overnight.

Check. Trace the asset and liability principal allocated across buckets back to opening balances. Read the net gap as asset repricing minus liability repricing.

Repricing implementation ↗
DAY 07Oct 17 · Sat

Verify units and conventions

Use basis points for shocks, decimal rates in valuation, years for curve tenors and CAD for the balance sheet. Define ΔEVE as shocked value minus base value and present a loss as a positive amount for the capital screen.

Check. Evaluate a zero shock. Confirm that baseline and shocked results coincide and that the unshocked delta is zero within rounding tolerance.

Model tests ↗

WEEK 02 / Oct 18–Oct 24

Value the six curve shocks

Build the runoff cash flows, apply the prescribed curve shapes and compare the largest adverse ΔEVE with capital.
DAY 08Oct 18 · Sun

Model scheduled mortgage principal

Generate payment-period cash flows for the fixed-rate mortgage book. Distinguish amortization from contractual renewal and model the outstanding principal due by the end of the remaining term.

Check. Reconcile cumulative scheduled principal, prepayment and the final payoff to the opening balance.

Cash-flow engine ↗
DAY 09Oct 19 · Mon

Introduce voluntary prepayments

Express annual constant prepayment rate (CPR) as a monthly conditional probability of 1 − (1 − CPR)^(1/12). Apply that probability to eligible mortgage principal. Faster repayments move principal forward and reduce its rate duration.

Check. Test a zero CPR and a faster CPR, and verify that interest and principal schedules remain internally consistent.

Behavioral cash-flow engine ↗
DAY 10Oct 20 · Tue

Separate core and non-core deposits

Give core balances a behavioral runoff horizon and let the non-core component reprice faster. Keep deposit decay distinct from deposit beta, which describes how much of a market-rate move reaches the customer rate.

Check. Sweep the decay assumption and check how the deposit contribution to economic value and the repricing ladder changes.

Deposit runoff and bank assumptions ↗
DAY 11Oct 21 · Wed

Build the six prescribed curves

Calculate parallel up and down, short up and down, steepener and flattener scenarios. Weight the short component with exp(−t/4), and weight the long component with one minus that factor. Apply the rotation coefficients and the national floor.

Check. At long tenors, confirm that short shocks fade and the long component dominates rotations. Cross-check the CAD magnitudes and formulas with the source tables.

BCBS SRP31 shock specification ↗
DAY 12Oct 22 · Thu

Full-reprice each scenario

Discount the scenario’s asset, liability and hedge cash flows at the shocked term structure. Show the change against the corresponding unshocked EVE on a common sign convention.

Check. Review a bond’s direction under an upward move and check a zero-shock valuation before comparing the aggregate bank.

EVE valuation engine ↗
DAY 13Oct 23 · Fri

Evaluate the outlier threshold

Find the largest loss across the six prescribed EVE scenarios and divide by illustrative Tier 1 capital. Compare the result with the 15% benchmark. The reference book loses CAD 1.207B under parallel up, or 17.24% of illustrative Tier 1. Keep user-scaled shocks separate from the prescribed screen.

Check. Make the worst case traceable to its scenario. The modeled threshold comparison is a research screen for this synthetic book.

OSFI B-12 outlier test and Annex 1 ↗
DAY 14Oct 24 · Sat

Publish the reference scenario pack

Run the model from the pinned inputs and preserve reference scenario outputs in the repository. Keep the shock shape, opening assumptions, swap notional and units available alongside the values.

Check. Compare report output with the website’s default presets. Reprice the same book when comparing shocks so the difference has an interpretable source.

Published unhedged scenario pack ↗

WEEK 03 / Oct 25–Oct 31

Project earnings and hedge behavior

Reprice interest income and expense over twelve months, then test how behavioral and hedge assumptions move both measures.
DAY 15Oct 25 · Sun

Define the constant-size earnings view

Hold modeled book sizes constant for the 12-month earnings comparison. When principal runs off or matures, replace it within its product class under the model’s rollover convention.

Check. Confirm that replacement balances keep the simulated book size stable. Keep the runoff EVE calculation separate from this replenished earnings book.

NII projection engine ↗
DAY 16Oct 26 · Mon

Reprice floating assets and funding

Use short reference rates and assumed spreads to update floating-rate loans and borrowing as they reset. Keep fixed-rate coupons until the cash flows reprice or turn over.

Check. Check that an immediate short-rate move reaches floating positions before most fixed-rate mortgage balances reprice.

NII projection engine ↗
DAY 17Oct 27 · Tue

Apply deposit beta to customer pricing

Use a deposit beta to pass through a fraction of a reference-rate shock to the customer deposit rate. A beta of 40% implies 40 bp of rate response to a 100 bp reference-rate change before any modeled floors.

Check. Compare zero and higher pass-through, and verify that higher beta increases deposit expense under upward rate stress.

Deposit pricing assumptions ↗
DAY 18Oct 28 · Wed

Compare instant and ramped paths

Apply the full shock at the start in one earnings path and phase in the same endpoint across twelve months in the other. Accumulate monthly NII changes against an unshocked baseline.

Check. Check that the paths share the endpoint shock but differ in timing. Compare cumulative earnings over the entire horizon, where earlier repricing matters.

Run the scenario ↗
DAY 19Oct 29 · Thu

Size a pay-fixed hedge

Add a pay-fixed, receive-floating swap to offset part of the fixed-rate asset exposure. Use the same opening reference curve to set the fixed swap rate and compare hedge sizes against the unhedged book.

Check. Check the swap’s opening PV and the direction of its marked value in a parallel-up shock. Keep the hedge notional and the selected shock explicit.

Swap valuation engine ↗
DAY 20Oct 30 · Fri

Sweep assumptions one at a time

Vary CPR, core deposit decay, deposit beta and swap notional while keeping the rest of the assumptions fixed. Compare the impact on ΔEVE, 12-month ΔNII and the relevant bucket gaps.

Check. Separate changes to the original book from changes to the shocked-versus-base comparison. Attribute each result to the parameter changed.

Sensitivity controls ↗
DAY 21Oct 31 · Sat

Compare earnings and valuation trade-offs

Hold the reference assumptions fixed and add a CAD 10B pay-fixed swap. The worst EVE loss falls from 17.24% to 9.49% of Tier 1 and shifts to the steepener. The parallel-down 12-month earnings loss rises from CAD 148.0M to CAD 348.3M.

Check. Compare the complete shock pack at each hedge size. Review downside earnings as a separate measure from the Tier 1 EVE screen.

Animate the model path

Check. Confirm that the sample endpoint matches the report. The ramped parallel-up sample ends at −CAD 374.0M ΔEVE and +CAD 115.5M cumulative ΔNII.
